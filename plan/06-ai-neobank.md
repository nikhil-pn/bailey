# Bailey — AI-Native Neobank Reframing (adopted 2026-07-20)

**User decision:** Bailey is **the AI agents' neobank — run by an AI agent**. The neobank framing
stands even though Catena Labs markets "banking for AI agents" (their layer is human-governed
custody/policy, pre-transaction; Bailey is the agent-run neobank: accounts, statements, and the
close, post-transaction). A 3-lens panel (judging fit / scope / positioning, with a live
competitive check) returned unanimous **adopt-as-reframing**; the mechanic below is now core
product.

## What "the neobank is itself an AI" means concretely

Bailey is not a dashboard with an AI categorizer bolted on. **Bailey is an autonomous banker
agent**: it has its own Circle SCA wallet and ERC-8004 on-chain identity, it is *employed and
paid in USDC by the fleet*, and it acts on real on-chain signals. Nobody ships this (checked live
2026-07-20: Catena = human control over agent money; ClawBank "Manfred" = novelty agent-with-
account; Pilot/Basis = autonomous bookkeepers for human firms, off-chain).

**The signature demo beat** (the one judges will retell): the fleet funds a "July close" job in
the live ERC-8183 escrow → Bailey delivers the statement (artifact hash) → `PaymentReleased`
pays Bailey net-of-fee → **Bailey's own fee appears, correctly categorized, as a line in the very
statement it just produced** — generated by the same un-special-cased pipeline. The banker keeps
its own books.

## The decision gates (mandatory once this framing is adopted)

These are the answer to the killer Q&A question *"what does Bailey actually decide?"* — without
them the reframe deflates to "cron + LLM":

1. **Pending-escrow hold** — period ended but ERC-8183 jobs still escrowed → journal: "holding close."
2. **Reconciliation gate** — refId reconciliation vs Circle List-Transactions fails → Bailey refuses to sign off.
3. **Confidence escalation** — categorizations below threshold are escalated, not auto-booked.

All gate decisions are persisted to a visible **decision journal** in the UI.

## Guardrails (kept from the panel, cheap and load-bearing)

- **Bailey never moves fleet funds.** It observes, reports, delivers, and gets *paid* (fleet side
  releases). Auditor independence — "your bookkeeper should not hold your money" — is a
  compliance feature AND the clean deconfliction line vs the friend's spend-control project.
- **Disclaimer line in deck/site:** "Bailey is software, not a bank or custodian — funds never
  leave your fleet's Circle wallets." (Immunizes the FDIC/Chime-style "you're not a licensed
  bank" question in one sentence.)
- Honest Q&A answers, rehearsed: (1) *what could Bailey have decided differently?* → the three
  gates above, with journal entries; (2) *isn't the fleet paying yourself?* → counterparties are
  simulated (every hackathon demo's are), the MECHANISM is real: live escrow, delivery-
  conditioned release, net-of-fee accounting.
- Anomaly detection described as exactly what it is: threshold rule + LLM triage with ArcScan
  evidence links.

## Positioning copy

- Headline: **"Bailey — the neobank for AI-agent fleets, run by an AI agent."**
- Support: *"Bailey is the banker your agent fleet employs — an autonomous AI agent with its own
  wallet and on-chain identity that watches every USDC movement, closes the books on schedule,
  and earns its fee in the very statement it just produced."*
- Unchanged: one-liner ("Your agents do the business. Bailey keeps the books.") and market claim
  ("No one closes the books for agent fleets.")
- vs Catena (contrast slide, their live language): Catena = "Agent Action, Human Control" —
  humans govern agents' money, custody/policy. Bailey = the agent-run neobank for what happened
  to the money: accounts, statements, close. Different layer; Bailey is also *itself* one of the
  agents.
